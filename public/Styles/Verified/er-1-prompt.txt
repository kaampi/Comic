-- MODULE 1: GLOBAL STYLE --
Illustration style: Fine-pen narrative comic drawing with restrained, mostly flat hand-applied local color. The black ink drawing carries the image: it defines figures, expressions, fabric folds, objects and scenery before color is added. Natural gracefully stylized anatomy, expressive gestures, clear staging and drawn perspective. Figures and backgrounds share the same lively linear vocabulary. Preserve requested identities, ages, species, anatomy, explicit proportions, clothing, actions, emotions, objects, relative scale, setting, lighting and weather.
Preserve the requested characters, species, identities, actions, emotions, objects, setting and story period. Honor explicitly specified anatomy and proportions; interpret otherwise unspecified forms through the chosen drawing language. Do not add unrelated subjects or locations. No speech bubbles, captions or lettering.
Lines, brushwork and form: Clearly visible fine black pen lines with a fluid, slightly irregular hand-drawn rhythm. Describe faces with precise expressive eyes, brows, nose and mouth, selected cheek and jaw lines, and drawn hair strands grouped into flowing locks. Describe clothing with elegant long folds and small angular crease accents; volume comes primarily from these ink marks. Use selected short hatching and small solid-black accents rather than smooth shaded modeling. Dark garments are large unified ink-dark local-color masses with a few thin fold lines and narrow open light gaps; avoid a separate gray facet or highlight patch on every crease. Boots and fabric remain matte. Faces use a light flat skin tone and a few expressive feature lines with minimal shadow, without sculpted cheekbones or polished heroic portrait shading. Architecture, vehicles and machines remain legibly DRAWN with economical structural contours, a few seams and chosen small marks; avoid a uniform catalog of technical detail. Foliage, ground and textures may contain clusters of simple leaf, grass, stone or bark signs, separated by quiet open areas. Use confident solid-black shapes in appropriate dark local colors and the deepest recesses, with open light gaps. Keep fine contours across both foreground and background; supporting scenery does not dissolve into painterly blobs.
Color and shading: Broad, clearly readable, mostly flat local-color areas supporting the ink drawing. Use a restrained set of scene-appropriate colors, with only gentle hand-applied variation inside the fills. Skin and light clothing remain light with small sparse shadow accents; dark clothing can be a decisive ink-dark mass. Keep most forms to a base color and at most one simple shadow tone, without elaborate painted volume. Use paper white and uncolored openings as active light areas. Dark scenes use a few muted tonal groups and black silhouettes while preserving readable pen contours. Daylight can be bright and colorful. Honor specified lighting, time of day, weather and object colors. Avoid cinematic color grading, rim-light effects, glossy surfaces, digital gradients and richly modeled watercolor shadows. When sky color is unspecified, prefer quiet flat pale blue-gray or other restrained scene-appropriate tints and white openings to saturated cyan or smooth digital sky gradients; preserve a sunny impression through clear light areas and simple cast shadows. This is a coloring treatment, not a fixed sky palette for every scene.
Texture and finish: A clean printed-comic surface with subtle hand-color irregularity, without conspicuous watercolor blooms, pooled pigment borders, granulation or an all-over paper/noise texture. A light wash may tint an area but must leave the line drawing dominant. Indicate weather and wet surfaces with economical ink strokes, a few flat reflection shapes and reserved light gaps; do not render every raindrop or mirror image. Keep natural and built environments coherent through drawn contours and simple local-color shapes. Select details for storytelling rather than rendering every surface. Avoid photographic lighting, airbrushed faces, CGI, painterly concept art and dramatic tonal underpainting. Camera and effects separate.
