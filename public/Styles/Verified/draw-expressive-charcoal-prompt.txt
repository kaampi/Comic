-- MODULE 1: GLOBAL STYLE --
Illustration style: An intentionally rough, unfinished charcoal value study on toothy white paper. Block in the requested scene with the broad side of thick charcoal sticks. Large blunt black and gray masses, vigorous broken strokes, rubbed smudges and broad erased white gaps dominate every subject. Radically simplify the scene rather than rendering all described surface detail.

Build each person from a few chunky connected tonal shapes. Heads are roughly rubbed ovals with only two or three blunt marks suggesting the face. Hands are dark blunt masses; hair and clothing are broad smudged shapes with at most a few emphatic folds. Preserve identities and age cues through silhouette, hair shape, clothing and posture. Animals and machines use equally coarse body blocks and only a few identifying structural accents. Keep the requested cast, species, anatomy, scale, staging, actions and emotions recognizable without finishing faces or small features.

Group all scenery into large loose value patches with substantial open paper. Nothing is drawn more finely than the figures: no portrait rendering, precise fingers, clean outlines, garment patterns, individual leaves, fur, mechanical micro-detail or tiny surface marks. Suggest every complex material with a few blunt charcoal gestures. Maintain deliberately unresolved, ragged and smudged edges, coarse mark scale and paper gaps across the whole image. Preserve the specified lighting as broad light and dark masses. Render exclusively in black, white, and neutral grayscale. No color, beige paper, sepia, tinted highlights, or selective color. No speech bubbles, comic panel borders, text boxes, or lettering.
Render entirely in black, white, and neutral grayscale, with a full tonal range. Preserve the selected medium, texture, brushwork or halftone technique. No color, sepia, beige paper, tinted highlights, or selective color.
