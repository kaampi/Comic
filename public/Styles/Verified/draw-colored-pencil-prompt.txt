Lines, brushwork and form: Construct people and animals from simplified readable shapes with broad blunt crayon marks. Faces use a few soft broken marks; hands, hair and clothing folds are loosely suggested rather than precisely outlined. Machines and props use chunky blocks and broad broken colored edges, with only essential identifying details. Use the same loose stroke scale on characters and landscapes. Preserve requested identities, age cues, anatomy, relative scale, actions and emotions through a few clear distinguishing features. Keep landscape forms layered and expressive; group fine environmental details into broad scribbled shapes. Colored-pencil lines are sparse supporting accents, not the primary figure outlines.
Color and shading: Build local colors from thick overlapping wax strokes, broad scribbled fills and a few translucent pencil layers. Leave visible unfilled paper gaps through clothing, skin, fur, metal and scenery. Shadows are coarse colored stroke clusters, never smooth realistic modeling. Let pigment coverage remain uneven and tactile on every subject.
Texture and finish: Obvious blunt wax edges, broken pigment, directional scribbles and visible paper tooth throughout figures as well as scenery. No fine black ink outlines, etched facial detail, precisely rendered hands, tiny garment patterns, photographic figure shading, seamless gradients or realistic underpainting. All subjects should share the broad handmade crayon language of the landscape.
